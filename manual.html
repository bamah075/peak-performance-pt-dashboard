Chapter 1 — Executive Summary

Onboarding is a systems problem, not a labour problem.

Today, bringing a new client onto GoHighLevel takes Undeniable Mentoring six to eight hours — rebuilding a pipeline, writing nurture copy, wiring automations, and building a landing page from scratch, every single time.

That's not a staffing gap. It's the absence of a template. This submission builds one, end to end, using Sarah Mitchell of Peak Performance PT as the proof case.

"Everything below is designed to be built once and cloned in under thirty minutes — the system is the deliverable, not just the client."

Chapter 4 — Automation, Live in GHL (continued)

The branch logic that does the real work.

Three-way branch: opened 2+ emails / clicked CTA / neither

Drip emails 4–5, then end of sequence

A workflow that only sends emails on a timer isn't really automation — it's a mail-merge with a delay. The branch logic is what makes it a system:

  • Opened 2+ emails → tag Behaviour:HighEngagement, so Sarah can see who's warm without reading a single email herself.
  • Clicked the CTA → move straight to Call Booked stage — no waiting on the timer once someone's ready.
  • No email open in 5 days → one re-permission SMS: "Did life get busy? No stress — your guide is still here when you are."

Chapter 4 — Automation, Live in GHL (continued)

Workflow 2: Discovery Call Management

Trigger: Appointment Status = Booked — reminders, prep, and a full no-show recovery path.

The happy path

  1. Confirmation email with prep questions (immediate)
  2. Prep SMS (5 min) + tag Status:CallBooked
  3. 24-hour reminder SMS — reply RESCHEDULE to bail out gracefully
  4. 2-hour final reminder with the meeting link
  5. Call completed → move to Call Completed, send recap email, create Sarah a task: "Send proposal within 24h"

The edge case — no-show

  1. Tag Behaviour:NoShow
  2. Wait 2 hours → warm reschedule SMS, no guilt-tripping
  3. Wait 48 hours → if still not rebooked, tag FollowUp:NeedsAttention and notify Sarah directly

Chapter 4 — Automation, Live in GHL (continued)

Workflow 3: the stagnation alert nobody asks for — until they need it.

Every pipeline eventually has leads that quietly stop moving. A contact gets tagged Status:CallBooked and then… nothing. No outcome logged, no follow-up, no visibility — until this workflow catches it.

"The best automations aren't the ones that fire the most. They're the ones that notice when something should have happened and didn't."

Trigger & logic

  1. Trigger: tag Status:CallBooked is added
  2. Wait 7 days
  3. If the pipeline stage is still Call Booked → tag FollowUp:NeedsAttention and fire an internal notification: "STUCK LEAD: [Name] has been in 'Call Booked' for 7 days with no movement."
  4. Otherwise → end silently, nothing to report

This is the exact scenario the Task 3 dashboard surfaces as a red flag — the automation and the dashboard are reading from the same signal, not two disconnected systems.

Chapter 6 — The AI Content Engine

Two prompts. Anyone on the team can run them.

Prompt 1 builds the strategy. Prompt 2 writes the copy. Nobody needs to be a prompt engineer — they fill in six brackets and paste.

Prompt 1 — Strategy Brief

ROLE: You are a senior conversion copywriter specialising in Australian fitness coaching. You write warm, empowering copy for women aged 28–45 who are frustrated with group fitness and want personalised attention. CONTEXT: - Business: [CLIENT_BUSINESS_NAME] - Location: [CLIENT_LOCATION] - Offer: [CLIENT_PROGRAM_NAME] at [CLIENT_PRICE] - Target Audience: [CLIENT_AUDIENCE] - Lead Magnet: [CLIENT_LEAD_MAGNET] - Brand Tone: Empathetic, confident, jargon-free, "big sister energy" — not drill sergeant TASK: Create a content strategy brief that includes: 1. The #1 emotional pain point this audience wakes up with 2. The #1 objection they have to buying personal training 3. 3 "voice anchors" — phrases this brand should use repeatedly 4. A "forbidden words" list (gym jargon to avoid) 5. The transformation story arc for a 5-email nurture sequence OUTPUT FORMAT: Markdown. Be specific. No generic advice.

Prompt 2 — Content Generator (run after reviewing Prompt 1's output)

Using the strategy brief above, generate for [CLIENT_BUSINESS_NAME]: === PART A: 5-EMAIL NURTURE SEQUENCE === Each email: subject line (≤50 chars), preview text (≤90 chars), 150–250 words, 1 CTA (soft in emails 1–3, direct in 4–5), references the lead magnet naturally, Australian English spelling. === PART B: LANDING PAGE COPY === Headline (≤12 words) · Subheadline · 4 transformation-focused bullets · CTA button text (≤4 words) · Form headline OUTPUT FORMAT: Clean markdown. Label each section clearly.

Chapter 6 — The AI Content Engine (continued)

What Prompt 1 actually surfaced.

The pain point

"I'm trying so hard, but nothing seems to stick."

She doesn't believe she's lazy — she believes something is wrong with her. The real enemy isn't fitness. It's the belief that she can't trust herself anymore.

The objection

"I don't want to spend $3,000 and fail again."

Price is rarely the real objection — the fear is wasting money on something that won't stick, again.

Three voice anchors

  • "We're building something you can actually keep."
  • "Your plan should fit your life, not the other way around."
  • "You're not starting over. You're starting smarter."
